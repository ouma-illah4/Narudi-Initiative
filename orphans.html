The Reality in Kenya

In Kenya, thousands of children grow up without parental care due to poverty, disease, and social challenges. Many are found within informal settlements in cities like Nairobi, where access to basic needs remains limited.

It is estimated that **over 2.6 million children in Kenya are orphans or vulnerable**, with a significant number living in conditions that expose them to hunger, lack of education, and emotional distress.

Behind each number is a child facing uncertainty—without consistent support, guidance, or opportunity.

Key Challenges

2.6+ Million Orphans & Vulnerable Children in Kenya
Many Live in Informal Settlements
Limited Access to Education & Healthcare
High Risk of Poverty & Exploitation

These challenges are often intensified by unemployment, rising living costs, and limited social protection systems.

Why This Is Happening

  • Poverty: Many families struggle to provide basic needs such as food, shelter, and education.
  • Disease: Illnesses continue to claim lives, leaving children without caregivers.
  • Urban Challenges: Rapid urbanization in Nairobi increases pressure on already strained resources.
  • Family Instability: Social and economic stress can lead to broken family structures.

The Impact on Children

Without proper support, orphaned children are more likely to drop out of school, face malnutrition, and experience emotional trauma. Many grow up without mentorship or guidance, limiting their future opportunities.

However, with the right support system, these children can overcome these challenges and build meaningful lives.

What We Do at Narudi Initiative

  • Provide access to education and school support
  • Offer food assistance and basic necessities
  • Create safe and supportive environments
  • Mentorship and emotional support programs
  • Work with communities to protect vulnerable children

A Global Perspective

The challenge is not limited to Kenya. Globally, over **150 million children** are classified as orphans. Every day, thousands more lose parental care due to disease, conflict, and poverty.

Addressing this issue locally contributes to a larger global effort to protect children and secure their future.
